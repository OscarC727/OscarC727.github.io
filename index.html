Now

I lead the control team for the IEEE EXO project, a lower-limb exoskeleton built with USF's CARRT Lab for balance correction during perturbed walking. My side covers gait-phase detection, the actuator control loop, and a sensing network on a CAN bus.

I also run PID workshops for the control team, and I'm leading us through building a rotary inverted pendulum as shared practice before we tune the real thing. The simulation above is a small version of that exercise.

On my own time I'm learning Verilog on an FPGA, working toward my first digital design project.

Projects

  • Audio spectrum analyzer

    Real-time FFT visualizer that listens through a microphone and draws the spectrum on a TFT display. I debugged the whole path from mic to FFT to screen.

    Teensy 4.0, ILI9341 display, C++

  • Self-stabilizing spoon

    A tremor-cancelling spoon on a two-axis servo gimbal. An IMU measures the hand's motion and a PID loop drives the servos to hold the spoon level. This is where I learned filtering and PID from scratch.

    ESP32 and XIAO ESP32, MPU6050, SG90 servos, C++

  • Rotary inverted pendulum in progress

    The EXO control team's shared testbed for tuning PID, with LQR experiments planned next.

  • FPGA and Verilog in progress

    First steps into digital logic design, working down the stack toward how chips are built.
